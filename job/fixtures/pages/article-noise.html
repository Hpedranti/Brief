Fed governor Ana Hale backs one more rate cut this year

By Fixture Markets Staff · Updated 12:15 PM ET

Federal Reserve Governor Ana Hale said on Thursday that cooling inflation leaves room for one more quarter-point interest rate cut before the end of the year, in a speech to a business group in Cleveland.

“The data since the summer have been encouraging,” Hale said, adding that price growth for services had slowed for three straight months. She said the labor market was “cooling, not cracking” and that she saw no sign of a sharp rise in layoffs.

Hale, who joined the Fed board in 2024, is seen by investors as a centrist on the committee. Her remarks were the clearest signal yet from a board member that a cut at the December meeting is likely, according to analysts quoted by Fixture Markets.

Not every official agrees. Two regional bank presidents said last week that they wanted to see more months of data before cutting again, and minutes from the September meeting showed "several" participants worried that inflation could stall above the 2 percent target.

Market reaction

Yields on two-year Treasury notes fell about five basis points after the speech, and futures markets priced a higher chance of a December cut. Mortgage rates, which track longer-term yields, were little changed on the day.

The Fed's next policy meeting is scheduled for October 27–28. Hale did not say whether she would support a move at that meeting.
