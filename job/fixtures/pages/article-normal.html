Northland and Southmark agree to talks on extending Vell Valley truce

October 15, 2026 · 11:40 AM GMT · By Fixture Reporter

Negotiators from Northland and Southmark said on Thursday that they will meet next week in the neutral city of Arlen to discuss turning a 30-day ceasefire in the Vell Valley into a longer agreement, according to statements released by both foreign ministries.

The truce, which began on September 20, has largely held despite what monitors described as “sporadic small-arms fire” near the river crossings. A spokesperson for the Northland foreign ministry said the talks would focus on a withdrawal line for heavy weapons and on reopening two road crossings that have been closed since the spring.

What each side wants

Southmark’s deputy foreign minister, Ilse Varga, told reporters that her government wanted international observers to stay in the valley — a demand Northland has resisted in the past. "We are ready to talk about anything that keeps people safe," Varga said.

Northland officials said they would bring proposals on prisoner exchanges and on the return of about 12,000 displaced residents. The ministry did not say whether it would accept observers.

Shipping companies that use the Vell estuary have been watching the talks closely. Traffic through the estuary fell by roughly a third during the fighting this summer, according to figures from the regional port authority, and insurers raised premiums for vessels calling at nearby ports.

What to watch

The 30-day ceasefire expires on October 20. Diplomats from both countries said a short extension could be agreed at the start of next week’s meeting to give negotiators more time, but neither side committed to one. The talks are expected to last three days.
